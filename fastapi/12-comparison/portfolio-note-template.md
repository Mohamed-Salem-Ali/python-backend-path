# Blog note: Django vs FastAPI, what I learned (template)

Copy this file into your portfolio repo, and write the note in your own words. Aim for about 800 words. Each heading has guidance in italics: replace the guidance with your text, and delete it when you are done. Read [the comparison](comparison.md) first, so that your note adds your own experience to it rather than repeating it.

**Title:** _Name the situation, not the verdict. For example, "Django or FastAPI for a savings-circle app: what I would pick again". Avoid "the ultimate guide"._

**Published:** _date_ **Read time:** _about N minutes_ **Repository:** _URL_ **Live:** _URL_

## 1. Why I wrote this (about 80 words)
_One sentence per project: what you built, with which framework, and what you were trying to find out by building it twice._

## 2. The two projects (about 120 words)
_Describe the scope honestly. Name the features that exist in one project and not the other, so a reader can see that size and test counts follow the scope. Give your test counts, and the command you used to count them._

## 3. One place the framework decided for me (about 150 words)
_Pick one behavior from the comparison, such as lazy loading, object permissions, background work or the generated docs. Show it with a short example from your own code. Say what the framework did, and what you had to do yourself._

## 4. One thing that surprised me (about 120 words)
_Something that was easier, or harder, than you expected. Be specific, and say how you found out: a failing test, a log line, or a query count._

## 5. Where I would pick each (about 150 words)
_Two short paragraphs, one for Django and one for FastAPI. Describe a situation, not a slogan: "For an app where staff edit records every day, I would pick Django, because the admin does that work for me."_

## 6. What I did not measure (about 60 words)
_Say what you did not test: speed under load, the cost of each host, or how each one ages over several years. A clear limit makes the rest of the note easier to trust._

## 7. What I would do next (about 40 words)
_One or two concrete steps, such as a queue for the background work, or a status route for each job._

---

**Before you publish:** every number matches a command you ran, and you can show that command; no secret, token, password or personal data appears in the text, the samples or the screenshots; the links open; the note does not claim a speed result you did not measure.
